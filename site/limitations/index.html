Risks and limitations

Last updated November 24, 2026

My Tasks is free and open source, and ready for use within the limitations below. Please read this page before you rely on it. When you set up My Tasks, you're asked to confirm you've read it.

1. Bugs and features still to come

My Tasks is ready for use, but the features on the Roadmap aren't built yet. Every change is tested, but like any software it can have bugs, including ones that lose or change data.

What to do: save a backup file from time to time (Settings › Backup) and keep it somewhere safe.

2. GitHub account and token

My Tasks has no server or accounts of its own. Your tasks are kept in a secret gist in your GitHub account. A gist is a file stored in your GitHub account; a secret gist isn't listed or searchable, but anyone with its web address can open it, which is why My Tasks encrypts it. So you need a free GitHub account and a personal access token. A token with the gist permission can read and change all of your gists, not only My Tasks'. Each device stores its token in the browser, unencrypted.

What to do: use a GitHub account where that's acceptable, give the token an expiry, such as 90 days, and revoke the token on GitHub if a device is lost.

3. Your passphrase

Your tasks are encrypted with a passphrase you choose. My Tasks doesn't store it or send it anywhere, so it can't be recovered. If you forget it and no device is still connected, your data is lost.

Each change to your gist keeps the older version in the gist's history, including tasks you've since deleted, encrypted with the passphrase in use at the time. Changing your passphrase doesn't re-encrypt them.

What to do: choose a strong passphrase from the start, don't use it anywhere else, and keep it in a password manager. A device that's still connected can set a new one.

4. Editing on two devices

Devices sync through your gist a few seconds after each change. Changes to different tasks on two devices, and to different parts of the same task (such as one device completing it and another adding a note), are all kept once each device has synced. If the same part of a task is changed on two devices before they sync, such as its title on both, the later change wins, judged by each device's clock, and the other change is lost; the device whose change was lost tells you which tasks were affected. A change that hasn't synced yet is only on its device, so it's lost if that device's data is cleared or it is disconnected first. How changes on two devices are kept.

What to do: let a device sync (the sidebar shows "Synced") before switching to another.

5. Sharing with someone else

Two or more people who trust each other can share My Tasks by using one gist, one token and one passphrase. The more people who share it, the more likely conflicts and surprises become: changes replacing each other, and shared settings or actions such as Empty Trash affecting everyone. There's no privacy between you: each of you can see, change and delete every task, and Focus on this Area only filters the screen. You all use the same token from the GitHub account that holds the gist, and it can read and change every gist in that account. Synced settings, tags and contexts are shared. Empty Trash, Archive, Restore and Change Passphrase affect all of you. When two of you change the same part of a task before syncing, only the person whose change was replaced is told. The gist's history keeps earlier versions, and anyone with the gist's address and the passphrase used at the time can open them. Sharing isn't suited to teams: there are no separate sign-ins or permissions, and no record of who changed what. How to share.

What to do: share only with people you'd trust with all your tasks and all the gists in that GitHub account. Give the token an expiry. When sharing ends, make a new token for your own devices and revoke the old one. Agree on who runs Empty Trash, Archive and Restore, and save a backup from time to time.

6. Several gists

A device can hold several gists, such as Work and Personal (Guide), but only one is open at a time, and nothing works across them: search, Today and the other lists show only the open gist, and tasks can't be moved from one gist to another. Each gist has its own 5,000-task limit, its own 170-day rule (a gist not synced on a device for over 170 days waits there, as a device does), and its own passphrase. A gist's name is in its description on GitHub, in plain text, where anyone with the gist's web address can read it.

What to do: split your tasks where you rarely need both at once, for example a gist for a large project; this is also how to go past 5,000 tasks. Use a different passphrase for each gist, and a name you don't mind being readable.

7. Your device's copy

Each device keeps a working copy of your tasks in the browser's storage so it works offline. That copy is not encrypted: anyone who can use your unlocked device can read it.

A backup file you save in Settings › Backup is not encrypted either: anyone who can open it can read your tasks. Restoring one replaces all your tasks, on every device once it syncs, and can't be undone.

Archive files and a Things export are plain CSV files, not encrypted either. Archive removes the items it saves from every device for good: the file is then the only copy.

What to do: lock your devices, and use Disconnect This Device in Settings before giving one away. Keep backup, archive and export files somewhere only you can open, and save a backup before you restore one.

8. Safari tabs

In a Safari tab, Safari can delete a site's data after 7 days without use. Your tasks are also in your gist and come back when you connect again, but a change not yet synced would be lost. On iPhone and iPad, the installed app and Safari keep separate copies.

What to do: install My Tasks (Add to Home Screen, or Add to Dock on a Mac). Your tasks are also in your gist.

9. What a web app can't do on its own

Without a server, My Tasks itself can't send notifications. Reminders are coming through the helper you run on a Mac, Mac only for now: you'll give a task a reminder time, and the helper will add it to Apple Reminders, which alerts you on your iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Watch. Until then, for a task that needs an alert at a set time, add a reminder for it in your device's reminders app. Web apps also can't have widgets, Siri or Shortcuts actions, an Apple Watch app, or a Quick Entry shortcut that works from other apps. Sharing is limited to people who trust each other using one gist (Sharing with someone else).

Without a server, every device's tasks travel as one encrypted file in your gist, which holds about 5 MB. So My Tasks holds up to 5,000 tasks, counting completed tasks and the Trash. From 4,500 the sidebar says how many you have; at 5,000 new tasks are refused until you make room. A repeating task still makes its next copy, and tasks from your other devices still arrive.

A web app can't read your device's calendars. Calendar events come only from the helper you run on a Mac (Guide): without it, no events show, on any device. Events arrive when the helper runs (every 15 minutes by default) and the app next syncs, so a new event can take a while to appear. They're read-only, and there's no helper for Windows, Linux or a phone yet. Your events, with their titles, times and details, are kept in your gist, encrypted like your tasks, and the helper keeps your token and passphrase in your Mac's login Keychain.

What to do: empty the Trash and delete completed tasks you no longer need. Save a backup first if you want to keep them.

10. Platforms

My Tasks is tuned for Safari on Mac, iPhone and iPad, and for Chrome and Edge. Android works but isn't tested. Firefox can't install web apps. Screen readers and other assistive technology aren't supported yet. English only.

Tag shortcuts use Control on Apple devices. On other systems the browser keeps Control and a letter for itself, so they use Alt instead; there, Alt with 0 to 5 stays with priorities, and in a browser tab Alt+D and Alt+H stay with their own shortcuts.

11. No guarantees

My Tasks is provided as is, under the MIT licence, with no warranty and no guaranteed support or availability. Ask for help in r/mytasksfyi on Reddit, or report problems on GitHub.

Because My Tasks is open source, you can also host your own copy, or change it to suit you: see Your own copy. A copy you host is yours to look after.
