Privacy

Last updated November 25, 2026

My Tasks has no server and no accounts. The app doesn't send your tasks, your passphrase or your token to the project. This page explains what is stored, where, and who can see it.

What is stored, and where

WhereWhatWho can read it
Your device, in the browser's storageA working copy of your tasks and your settings, so My Tasks works offline; with several gists, one copy for each. Separately: this device's GitHub token, and each gist's id, name and encryption key.Anyone who can use your unlocked device. It is not encrypted.
Your GitHub account, in a secret gist described as "My Tasks" (or "My Tasks · " and its name), one for each set of tasksYour tasks, encrypted with your passphrase. Only the app's name, the file format, the last-updated time and the values needed to decrypt it with your passphrase (salt and IV) are in plain text, and the gist's name, in its description ("My Tasks · Work"), so the calendar helper can show which gist it's asking about.Someone with your passphrase. A secret gist isn't listed, but anyone with its web address can download the encrypted file. GitHub, and anyone with the gist's web address, can see that the gist exists, its size, when it changes, and the details above that are in plain text, its name included.
Your GitHub account, in the same gist, as a second fileOnly if you run the calendar helper on your Mac: your events from the calendars you chose, with their titles, times and any details they have (location, notes, link, attendees and their responses, organizer, alerts), encrypted with the same passphrase as your tasks. Each device keeps the last copy, still encrypted, so events show offline. A gist the helper has no passphrase for gets a small unencrypted note instead, saying to run the helper's setup again; it holds no events or names.The same as your tasks: someone with your passphrase. GitHub can see that the file exists, its size and when it changes.
Your GitHub account, in the same gist, as a third fileOnly if you choose a Reminders list in the calendar helper and tick a reminder there: the tasks ticked, each by its id (not its title), with the time it was ticked and, for a repeating task, the day it was for, encrypted with the same passphrase as your tasks. Each tick is removed once its task is completed, or after 30 days.Someone with your passphrase. GitHub, and anyone with the gist's web address, can see that the file exists, its size and when it changes.
Your Mac's login KeychainOnly if you set up the calendar helper: your GitHub token and the passphrase of each gist it writes to, so the helper can run on its own. The helper reads your calendars on your Mac and sends them only to your gist; with a Reminders list chosen, it also decrypts your tasks on your Mac to make their reminders.Anyone who can use your Mac while you're signed in to it.
Apple Reminders, in the account that holds the list you chose (usually iCloud)Only if you choose a Reminders list in the calendar helper: for each open task with a reminder, its title, the reminder's date, time and repeat, and its project's name. Apple Reminders keeps them there and brings them to your devices, like any other reminder.You, on your devices signed in to that account, and the account's provider. In iCloud, Apple encrypts reminders end to end only when Advanced Data Protection is turned on for your account; otherwise Apple holds the keys. A list kept in another account (synced with CalDAV) isn't end-to-end encrypted.
A backup file, wherever you save itOnly if you use Save Backup: your tasks and synced settings. Never your token, your passphrase or the gist's id.Anyone who can open the file. It is not encrypted.
An archive file, wherever you save itOnly if you use Archive: the completed tasks and projects it removed, as a CSV file.Anyone who can open the file. It is not encrypted.
A Things export file, on your MacOnly if you run the Things script: your open Things items, as a CSV file. The script reads Things on your Mac and sends nothing anywhere.Anyone who can open the file. It is not encrypted.

What My Tasks doesn't collect

  • No analytics, tracking, advertising or cookies, in the app or on this website.
  • No accounts with the project, and no email address.
  • The app connects only to itself and to GitHub's gist API. Its Content Security Policy blocks every other connection.

Other services

  • GitHub stores your gist and sees your requests to it, under the GitHub General Privacy Statement.
  • Cloudflare hosts this website and the app, so it sees each visitor's IP address and the pages requested, under the Cloudflare Privacy Policy. It never sees your tasks: they go only between your devices and your gist. The project looks only at Cloudflare's traffic totals, such as the number of pages requested and the countries they come from.

Deleting your data

  • From a device: Settings › Sync › Disconnect This Device (Disconnect This Gist, with more than one gist) removes that gist's list, token and key from the device. The gist is kept.
  • Everywhere: delete each "My Tasks" gist at gist.github.com, revoke the token at github.com/settings/tokens, then disconnect each device.

Contact

Questions about privacy: open an issue on GitHub. To report a security problem privately, see Security.
