Privacy

Last updated November 21, 2026

My Tasks has no server and no accounts. The app doesn't send your tasks, your passphrase or your token to the project. This page explains what is stored, where, and who can see it.

What is stored, and where

WhereWhatWho can read it
Your device, in the browser's storageA working copy of your tasks and your settings, so My Tasks works offline. Separately: this device's GitHub token, the gist's id and the encryption key.Anyone who can use your unlocked device. It is not encrypted.
Your GitHub account, in a secret gist named "My Tasks"Your tasks, encrypted with your passphrase. Only the app's name, the file format, the last-updated time and the values needed to decrypt it with your passphrase (salt and IV) are in plain text.Someone with your passphrase. A secret gist isn't listed, but anyone with its web address can download the encrypted file. GitHub can see that the gist exists, its size, when it changes, and the details above that are in plain text.
Your GitHub account, in the same gist, as a second fileOnly if you run the calendar helper on your Mac: your events from the calendars you chose, with their titles, times and any details they have (location, notes, link, attendees and their responses, organizer, alerts), encrypted with the same passphrase as your tasks. Each device keeps the last copy, still encrypted, so events show offline.The same as your tasks: someone with your passphrase. GitHub can see that the file exists, its size and when it changes.
Your Mac's login KeychainOnly if you set up the calendar helper: your GitHub token and passphrase, so the helper can run on its own. The helper reads your calendars on your Mac and sends them only to your gist.Anyone who can use your Mac while you're signed in to it.
A backup file, wherever you save itOnly if you use Save Backup: your tasks and synced settings. Never your token, your passphrase or the gist's id.Anyone who can open the file. It is not encrypted.
An archive file, wherever you save itOnly if you use Archive: the completed tasks and projects it removed, as a CSV file.Anyone who can open the file. It is not encrypted.
A Things export file, on your MacOnly if you run the Things script: your open Things items, as a CSV file. The script reads Things on your Mac and sends nothing anywhere.Anyone who can open the file. It is not encrypted.

What My Tasks doesn't collect

  • No analytics, tracking, advertising or cookies in the app.
  • No accounts with the project, and no email address.
  • The app connects only to itself and to GitHub's gist API. Its Content Security Policy blocks every other connection.

Other services

  • GitHub stores your gist and sees your requests to it, under the GitHub General Privacy Statement.
  • Cloudflare hosts this website and the app, so it sees each visitor's IP address and the pages requested, under the Cloudflare Privacy Policy. It never sees your tasks: they go only between your devices and your gist.
  • Statcounter counts visits to this website, not the app, so the project can see that the site is reachable and working. For each page visited it records the date and time, your IP address, browser, operating system, screen size, the page and the link you came from, and it sets a cookie to tell first visits from return visits, under the Statcounter privacy policy. It never sees your tasks: the app has no tracking of any kind.

Deleting your data

  • From a device: Settings › Sync › Disconnect This Device removes that device's list, token and key. Your gist is kept.
  • Everywhere: delete the "My Tasks" gist at gist.github.com, revoke the token at github.com/settings/tokens, then disconnect each device.

Contact

Questions about privacy: open an issue on GitHub. To report a security problem privately, see Security.
