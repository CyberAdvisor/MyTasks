Ways to organize

My Tasks works well on its own, but if you already follow a method, here's how to set it up for three popular ones. Each uses only what's in My Tasks today; the Guide explains every feature.

On this page
  1. Getting Things Done (GTD)
  2. PARA
  3. The Eisenhower matrix

Getting Things Done (GTD)

David Allen's method: get everything out of your head into a trusted system, decide the next action for each thing, do it in the right place, and review the whole system each week.

Set it up

  1. Areas for the parts of your life (Work, Home, Health), and a project for each outcome that takes more than one step.
  2. Contexts for where or how you can act: in Settings › Contexts, add @Home, @Computer, @Errands and @Phone, and a Waiting context for things you're waiting on from others.
  3. Make projects whose steps go in order sequential: in Settings › Sequential Projects, list a tag such as "next" with a priority and context, then tag those projects with it. Each keeps just its next action in Today.

Day to day

  • Capture: add everything to the Inbox, with Quick Entry (Ctrl Space) from anywhere in the app.
  • Clarify and organize: empty the Inbox by moving each task to a project or area and giving it a context. Something for later gets a start date and waits in Scheduled; a maybe goes to Deferred (GTD's Someday/Maybe).
  • Engage: in Today, choose Group By › Contexts from the ⋯ after the title, or use the bar under the title to see only the context you're in.
  • Weekly review: empty the Inbox; open Quick Find and choose All Projects to check each has a next action; look through Scheduled and Deferred; and save a search for your Waiting context to check what you're waiting on.

PARA

Tiago Forte's method sorts everything into four places: Projects (with a goal and an end), Areas (ongoing responsibilities), Resources (topics of interest) and Archives (anything finished or inactive).

Set it up

  1. Areas for your responsibilities (Health, Finances, Home, each client), each holding its projects.
  2. A Resources area for topics you're collecting, such as Recipes or Books to Read. Give its projects a start date of Deferred, so their tasks stay out of Today and Unscheduled until you pick one up.
  3. Tags for anything that cuts across all four, such as a person's name or a quarter's goal.

Day to day

  • Work from Today and the projects in your areas.
  • Archive a project when it's done: choose Complete Project from its ⋯ menu. Quick Find's Completed Projects shows them all, and Completed holds the finished tasks.
  • To shelve a project without finishing it, give it a start date of Deferred; bring it back by clearing it.

The Eisenhower matrix

Sort each task by whether it's urgent and whether it's important: do urgent, important tasks first; schedule important ones that aren't urgent; delegate urgent ones that aren't important; and eliminate the rest.

Set it up

  1. In Settings › Priority Settings, rename the levels: P1 Do first, P2 Schedule, P3 Delegate and P4 Eliminate. Leave P5 unused, or use it for "not sorted yet".
  2. Leave Set P1 automatically for due and overdue tasks in Today on: a task due today is urgent. Turn off Set P2 by default for tasks added to Today, so you choose each task's quadrant yourself.
  3. Use deadlines for real due dates, so urgent tasks come to Today on their own.

Day to day

  • In Today, choose Group By › Priorities from the ⋯ after the title: your tasks appear in the four quadrants, Do first at the top.
  • Give each new task its quadrant as you add it. Give Schedule tasks a start date; hand Delegate tasks on and keep them in a Waiting context; delete or complete Eliminate tasks rather than letting them pile up.
