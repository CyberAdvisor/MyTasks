Before you start

  • A free GitHub account. My Tasks keeps your tasks, encrypted, in a secret gist there: a file in your account that isn't listed or searchable.
  • Safari on iPhone or iPad (iOS 18.4 or later) or on a Mac (macOS 14 or later), or Chrome or Edge on Mac, Windows or Linux. Android works but isn't tested.
  • About five minutes for your first device.

Install My Tasks

Open mytasks.fyi/app. Installed, My Tasks opens in its own window, works offline and keeps its data safe. On iPhone and iPad, install it before you connect: the Home Screen app keeps its own copy, separate from Safari.

DeviceHow to install
iPhone, iPadIn Safari, tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen, then Add. Open My Tasks from your Home Screen.
Mac (Safari)Choose File › Add to Dock…, then Add. Open My Tasks from the Dock.
Chrome or EdgeClick Install My Tasks at the bottom of the sidebar, or the install button at the right of the address bar.
FirefoxFirefox can't install web apps. My Tasks works in a tab, or use Chrome, Edge or Safari.

Create a GitHub token

The first screen asks for a personal access token, which lets My Tasks keep your tasks in a gist in your account.

  1. Click How to get a token, then the link to GitHub's new token page, and sign in. It opens with the name "My Tasks" and the gist box ticked.
  2. Choose an expiry, such as 90 days. When it expires, the sidebar says the token isn't valid: make a new one and use Change Token in Settings › Sync on each device. It's used for every gist on that device that used the old token. GitHub also removes tokens that go unused for a year.
  3. Leave only gist ticked, then click Generate token.
  4. Copy the token (it starts with ghp_) and paste it into My Tasks.
  5. Read the risks and limitations, tick the box that says you have, and click Connect.

A token with the gist permission can read and change all of your gists, so give it an expiry. It stays on your device and is used only with GitHub.

Choose a passphrase

On your first device, choose a passphrase of at least 12 characters and type it twice. A password manager, or four random words, makes a good one. My Tasks encrypts your tasks with it before anything leaves your device, and creates a secret gist named "My Tasks".

Write it down or keep it in a password manager. It can't be recovered. If you forget it and no device is still connected, your data is lost.

Add another device

  1. Open mytasks.fyi/app on the device and install it.
  2. Paste a token: the same one, or a new one made the same way.
  3. My Tasks finds your "My Tasks" gist. Enter your passphrase.

Anything already on that device is merged in. From then on, each device syncs a few seconds after every change, and when you open it.

Changing the same task on two devices

Each device keeps its own copy of your tasks and syncs it with your gist a few seconds after every change. Changes to different tasks on different devices are all kept, and so are changes to different parts of the same task: whether it’s done, its title, its notes, its dates (start date, deadline and repeat), its priority, its context, its tags, and where it is (its area, project, heading and place in the list). Projects work the same way.

For example, John completes “Buy milk” at 9:00 while his phone is offline, and Jane adds a note to the same task at 10:00 while hers is online. When John’s phone syncs again, the task is completed and has Jane’s note, on both phones.

When the same part of a task is changed on two devices before they’ve synced, the later change is kept and the earlier one is lost, judged by each device’s clock. If both of you change the title, the later title wins. A task deleted on one device and changed on another goes by whichever happened later.

You’re told when it happens. The device whose change was replaced shows a notice after it syncs, such as “1 of your changes was replaced by a later change from another device”, listing each task or project and what was replaced (completed, title, notes, dates, priority, context, tags, checklist or place), or that it was deleted on another device. Click a task to open it and redo anything you need; OK closes the notice. The replaced change itself isn’t kept anywhere. You won’t see a notice when both devices made the same change, such as both completing a task, or for a priority My Tasks set by itself.

  • Let a device sync (the sidebar shows Synced) before switching to another. While a device is offline, the sidebar shows how many of its changes are waiting, such as Offline · 3 changes not synced.
  • Keep your devices’ clocks set automatically.

Sharing with someone you trust

People who trust each other, such as a couple or a household, can share one set of tasks. You all use the same gist, token and passphrase, so everyone can see and change everything. The more people who share it, the more likely it is that changes collide and replace each other, and that one person’s action, such as Empty Trash or a settings change, surprises the others. It isn’t made for teams: there are no separate sign-ins, no permissions and no record of who changed what. Read Sharing with someone else before you start.

Setting it up

  • Give each person an area of their own, such as “Michael” and “Jane”, and a shared one for household tasks if you like.
  • On each of their devices, install My Tasks, paste the same token you use, and enter your passphrase (Add another device).
  • Each of you chooses Focus on this Area for your own area. It only changes what you see: the others’ tasks are still there, a click away.

Working together

  • Changes to different tasks, and to different parts of the same task, are all kept (Changing the same task on two devices). When two of you change the same part before your devices sync, the later change wins, and only the person whose change was replaced sees the notice.
  • Synced settings are shared: Today’s grouping, the priority labels and the two automatic-priority switches, Completed Items, Sequential Projects, tasks shown per project in Unscheduled, and Show Calendar Events. So are tags and contexts: renaming or deleting one changes everyone’s tasks.
  • Some actions affect everything, for all of you: Empty Trash, Delete Immediately, Archive, Restore from Backup…, Move Completed Items and Change Passphrase. Agree that only one of you runs them.
  • Undo can refuse a step with “Can’t undo: changed on another device” when someone else’s device has touched that task since.
  • The 5,000-task limit counts everyone’s tasks.
  • If one of you runs the calendar helper on a Mac, its events show for all of you.

Stopping sharing

  • On GitHub, make a new token, and use Change Token in Settings › Sync on each of your own devices (it covers every gist on the device that used the old token). Then revoke the old token on GitHub (Settings › Developer settings › Personal access tokens). The other person’s devices stop syncing; they keep the tasks they already have until Disconnect This Device is used on them.
  • Change your passphrase in Settings › Sync.
  • Earlier versions in the gist’s history stay encrypted with the old passphrase: anyone who has the gist’s address and that passphrase can still open them.

Several gists

Your GitHub account can hold more than one My Tasks gist, each a separate set of tasks, such as Work and Personal. A device can connect to several of them and keeps its own copy of each. Each gist has its own tasks, projects, areas, tags and settings, its own passphrase, and a name and colour. Only one is open at a time, and only the open one syncs.

Adding a gist

  • In Settings › Sync › Your Gists, choose Add Gist…. It uses your current token; choose Use a different token only for a gist in another GitHub account.
  • Pick one of your other My Tasks gists and enter its passphrase, or create a new gist and choose its passphrase. Use a different passphrase from your other gists.
  • Give it a name (up to 24 characters, different from the device’s other gists) and one of six colours. The app then opens it.
  • To have the same gist on another device, choose Add Gist… there and pick it.

Switching, renaming and removing

  • With more than one gist connected on a device, a band under the sidebar’s header shows the open gist’s name in its colour. Click it to switch to another gist, add one, or manage them. On a phone the band is at the top of the lists, and each page shows the gist’s name under its title.
  • Settings › Sync › Your Gists lists your gists: Rename… changes the open gist’s name and colour on all your devices, and Switch opens another.
  • Disconnect This Gist removes only the open gist’s copy from this device and opens another one. The gist itself stays on GitHub. Removing the last one returns to Setup.

What’s kept per gist

  • Per gist: its tasks and settings, sync, the focused area, Backup, Archive and Import (the file names include the gist’s name), the 5,000-task limit, the 170-day rule and calendar events.
  • Per device, for all gists: the theme, and the sidebar’s width and whether it’s hidden.
  • Nothing works across gists: search, Today and the other lists show only the open gist, and tasks can’t be moved from one gist to another.
  • The calendar helper writes your events to every gist, each showing the calendars you chose for it. After adding a gist, run its setup again on your Mac.
  • A gist’s name is also in its description on GitHub, such as “My Tasks · Work”, in plain text. Its tasks stay encrypted.

More than 5,000 tasks

Each gist has its own 5,000-task limit, so splitting your tasks into separate gists gives you more room: for example, a gist for a large project or for an area of your life. Since nothing works across gists, split where you rarely need to see both at once.

Everyday use

  • Lists. Inbox holds new tasks. A task you move out of the Inbox while you're looking at it stays there, with its new date, under “1 task moved out of the Inbox” until you click OK or open another list. When you move one out from elsewhere, or add one that the list on screen can't show, a message says where it went, with Show to open that list. While a task is open for editing, it stays where it is in its list and moves when you close it. Today shows what's due today, Scheduled what's coming, Unscheduled what's open with no date, and Deferred what you've set aside. Completed and Trash hold the rest. Scheduled lists each day’s tasks by priority, with those that have none last. Unscheduled shows the first 3 tasks of each project or area; Show All under them shows the rest and Show 3 hides them again, and Settings › Unscheduled sets how many.
  • Adding tasks. Use the + button, or press N or Space in a list. Quick Entry (Ctrl Space) adds one to the Inbox from anywhere in the app.
  • Completing and canceling. Click a task’s checkbox to complete it; it stays in its list, struck through, for a moment. To cancel a task instead (it’s shown with an ✕), Alt-click its checkbox (Option-click on a Mac), or right-click it and choose Complete › Mark as Canceled. Uncheck a task to reopen it.
  • Checklists. Open a task and add a checklist; Return starts the next item, and pasting several lines adds one item per line. Drag the handle (≡) at the end of an item to move it; on iPhone and iPad it’s always there, and with a mouse it shows when you point at the item. To delete an item, erase its text and press Delete once more, or leave it empty: empty items go when the task closes.
  • Formatting notes. A task’s or project’s notes can have **bold**, *italic* (or _italic_), lists (start lines with - or 1. ) and links, either [label](https://…) or just an address such as https://… or www.…. Once you leave the notes they show formatted, and a click on a link opens it in a new tab. Click anywhere else on the notes to edit them as typed. Only web and email links become clickable. A checklist item can have links too, written the same way; they open when clicked, and a click elsewhere on the item edits it.
  • Start dates and deadlines. Give a task a start date and it moves to Today when that day comes. A deadline shows the days left and turns red when it's due; on that day the task moves into Today. A start date can't be later than the deadline. In either picker you can type the date in the field at the top and press Return: “tom”, “fri” (the next Friday), “next fri” (Friday of next week; weeks run Sunday to Saturday, as the calendar), “in 3 days”, “in 2 weeks”, “jan 12”, “12/25” or “2027-01-12”. To set a task aside for some day, choose Deferred at the bottom of the Start Date picker; choosing a date takes it out of Deferred again. On each task, a yellow star means it’s in Today, a grey date (such as “Fri” or “Dec 3”) means it’s scheduled for that day, and a dashed checkbox means it’s deferred; an unscheduled task has no mark.
  • Repeating tasks and projects. Tasks and projects can repeat on a schedule: see Repeating tasks and projects.
  • Sequential projects. In Settings › Sequential Projects, list a tag with a priority and a context. A project with that tag keeps one task in Today: when you complete it, the next task in the project moves in, with that priority and context, and shows its place, such as “Vacation · task 2 of 5”. Unchecking a task takes back the task it moved. If the next task has a later start date, or is Deferred, the project waits for it: nothing moves into Today until that date, and giving the task in Today a later date puts the project on hold the same way. If more than one of the project’s tasks ends up in Today, right-click the one to keep (or open it and tap ⋯) and choose Reset Other Tasks….
  • Areas, projects and headings. Create them with New List at the bottom of the sidebar. Drag tasks and projects to rearrange them; on iPhone and iPad, with a finger or an Apple Pencil, press and hold first, so a quick stroke never moves anything. Drag a task onto Inbox, Today, Scheduled, Unscheduled, Deferred or Trash in the sidebar to move it there; Scheduled asks for the date. A heading’s ⋯ menu can Archive it, which completes its tasks and moves them to Completed, or Convert to Project, which makes it a project of its own with its tasks. Deleting an area asks whether to keep its projects and tasks or move them to the Trash. A task’s second line names its project, and its heading after it, such as “Vacation : Packing”. The arrow beside an area in the sidebar, or a heading in a project, hides or shows what’s under it; your other devices keep the same. Drag the sidebar’s right edge to resize it; double-click the edge to reset it. To hide the sidebar, drag its edge all the way to the left, then pull the window’s left edge to the right to bring it back. On iPad, tap the sidebar button above a list’s title. Cmd+/ (Ctrl+/ on Windows) hides and shows it too.
  • Focus on an area. Right-click an area in the sidebar and choose Focus on this Area (on iPhone and iPad, open the area and tap ⋯). Every list, count and search then shows only that area’s projects and tasks, plus the Inbox, until you tap the ✕ on “Focus: area” or choose Leave Focus. Tasks you add to Today or Scheduled meanwhile go in that area. Each device keeps its own focus.
  • Sort by date. Choose Sort by Date in a project’s ⋯ menu to list its tasks that aren’t under a heading with the ones you can do now first, then those starting later by their start date, then deferred ones. Each heading has its own Sort by Date in its ⋯ menu. Tasks always stay under their heading. Choose it again for your own order. Your other devices keep the same.
  • Right-click menus. Right-click (or Control-click) a saved search, area or project in the sidebar for its commands, such as Duplicate Project or New Project, without leaving the page you’re on. Right-click a task for its menu; on iPhone and iPad, open the task and tap ⋯ for the same menu, and in the Trash, tap a task to put it back.
  • Several tasks at once. Cmd-click (Ctrl-click on Windows) tasks to select several, Shift-click to select every task between two, or hold Shift and press ↑ or ↓; Cmd A selects the whole list. The Move dialog has a filter box at the top: type part of an area’s, project’s or heading’s name to find it. Then right-click any of them to give them all a start date, project, tags, deadline, priority or context, complete them, duplicate them or delete them; the toolbar’s Start Date and Move, and the keyboard shortcuts, work on all of them too. One Undo takes the whole change back. Drag any of them to move them all together. Press Esc to keep just one selected. On iPhone and iPad, tap Select in the ⋯ menu after a list’s, project’s, area’s or saved search’s title, tap the tasks you want, then use the bar along the bottom: Start Date, Move, Delete, and ⋯ for priority, context, tags, deadline, completing and duplicating. Tap Done when you’re finished. Swiping a task from right to left starts selecting too, with that task selected; swiping it from left to right opens its start date.
  • Printing. Choose Print… in the ⋯ menu after a list’s, project’s, area’s or saved search’s title, or press Cmd+P (Ctrl+P), to print the page as you see it, with the date it was printed. The installed app on iPhone and iPad can’t print; print from a Mac, or from My Tasks in Safari.
  • Priorities. Set P1 to P5 on any task. In Settings you can rename the levels, and turn off the automatic P1 for due tasks and P2 for tasks added to Today. A priority you choose is never changed by them. Choosing None keeps a task in Today without a priority; once the task leaves Today, or comes back with a new start date, the automatic priorities apply to it again.
  • Contexts and tags. Add contexts (where or how something gets done) in Settings, and tags on any task. The bar under each list's title filters by tag. To see the tasks in one context, choose Group By › Contexts in Today's ⋯ menu, make a saved search with that context, or search for its name.
  • Nested tags. In Settings › Tags, drag a tag by its icon onto another to nest it there, such as Groceries under Errand, or use the tag’s ⋯ button (also on iPhone and iPad). Filtering by Errand then shows tasks tagged Groceries too; the bar shows “Errand…”, and selecting it adds a Groceries pill to narrow it down. Deleting a tag deletes the tags nested under it.
  • Tag shortcuts. In Settings › Tags, click a tag’s box and press a letter or digit, such as G. Then Control G adds the tag to the selected tasks, or removes it if they all have it, and Control Option G filters the list by it; press it again to go back to the tag it’s nested under, or to clear the filter. On Windows, use Alt and Alt Shift.
  • Today grouping. Click the ⋯ after the Today title and choose Group By: All, Projects, Priorities or Contexts. Drag a task into another group to give it that group’s priority, project or context.
  • Saved searches. Search with Quick Find (type, then press Return), then Save Search to keep it in the sidebar. Its ⋯ menu has Edit Search… to change its name, text, tag or context. Quick Find also opens three built-in lists: Deadlines, All Projects and Completed Projects. Type part of the name, such as “dead”.
  • Completed tasks stay in their list, struck through, until they move to Completed: daily by default, at midnight. In Settings › Completed Items choose Immediately, Daily or Manually. To move them yourself, choose Move Completed Items in the ⋯ menu after a list’s, project’s or area’s title, or right-click a task; it moves only what’s waiting in that list. The same settings can show waiting completed tasks at the bottom of their list, and checked checklist items at the bottom of their checklist. In Today, Hide after the ⋯ hides the completed tasks for now and becomes Show, which brings them back; it’s there only when Today has completed tasks, applies to that device, and they show again whenever the app opens.
  • Appearance. Settings › Appearance chooses Light, Dark, or System to follow your device. Each device keeps its own choice.
  • Undo and Redo. Press Cmd Z to undo your last change, and again to go further back, up to 10 changes; Shift Cmd Z redoes them. On touch devices, tap Undo in the message that follows a delete or move, and again for each change before it.
  • Sync. The bottom of the sidebar shows when your tasks last synced; click it to sync now. Settings › Sync lists Your Gists (with Add Gist…), has Sync Now, Change Token, Change Passphrase and Disconnect This Device (Disconnect This Gist with more than one), and shows what’s in your gist: how many tasks and projects are open, completed, in the Trash and deleted, and the file’s size against its 5 MB limit. To make room, Settings › Archive moves old completed items to a file. A device that hasn’t synced for over 170 days (an old iPad in a drawer, say) waits instead: Settings › Sync explains, and offers to save a backup and then replace its tasks with the ones in your gist. This keeps tasks deleted long ago on your other devices from coming back.

Repeating tasks and projects

A task or a whole project can repeat: a weekly review, a monthly bill, a trip you pack for every year. You set it up once, and My Tasks makes a fresh copy each time it comes round.

Making something repeat

Right-click a task (or open it and tap ⋯) and choose Repeat…. For a project, choose Repeat… in the ⋯ menu after its title, or right-click it in the sidebar. Then choose:

  • Regularly: every so many days; weeks, on the days you pick; months, on a date such as the 16th or the last day, or a day such as the 2nd Tuesday or the last Friday; or years, in the month you pick.
  • After completion: a set time after you finish the last copy, such as 2 weeks after.
  • When the first copy comes, and whether the repeat ends: never, after a number of copies, or on a date.
  • Whether each copy gets a deadline, and starts some days before it.

On iPhone, the same choices come in a Repeat screen laid out for touch.

Templates and copies

What you set to repeat becomes its template: the pattern each copy is made from. A repeating task shows a blue repeat icon and the date of its next copy. A repeating project shows a repeat icon in the sidebar, and its page says “You’re editing the template of a repeating project”, with its rule and next dates under the title; click them to change the rule.

Each copy is an ordinary task or project that you work on and check off as usual; a task’s copy has a small grey repeat icon. A project’s copy is the whole project: its notes, tags and area, its headings, and its tasks with their checklists, in the same order, all open and unchecked. Archived headings and tasks in the Trash are left out. In a copy, Show Repeating Task or Show Repeating Project takes you to its template.

A repeating project’s template keeps no dates or completions on its tasks, since those belong to each copy, and its tasks show only on its page, never in Today, Unscheduled, search or the counts. A repeating task inside it stops repeating when the project starts repeating, so it isn’t made twice.

When copies appear

  • Copies are made when you open My Tasks, at midnight, and when you come back to it, so each one is there on its day.
  • If My Tasks wasn’t opened for several of the dates, one copy is made, for the latest, so a week away doesn’t fill Today.
  • A repeat after completion waits: its next copy comes only once you’ve finished the last one. If you move that copy to the Trash instead, the next one is dated from that day.
  • Copies are shared across your devices. Two devices making the same copy end up with one.
  • A task’s copy appears in Today. A project’s copy appears in the sidebar; its tasks have no start dates of their own, so they’re listed in Unscheduled, as in any project, until you give them one.
  • Scheduled shows what’s coming: a repeating task on its next date, and a repeating project’s template on its next copy’s date, with how many tasks it has.

Working ahead

  • A repeating task: check off the template itself, or choose Complete › Mark as Completed, and its next copy is made and completed now, dated on its day; the template moves on to the date after. Mark as Canceled skips the next copy the same way. A repeat after completion counts its next date from today.
  • A repeating project: choose Complete Project on the template. Its next copy is made now, on its date, and completed, with its tasks completed or canceled as you choose, and the template moves on.
  • A task in a project’s template: checking it off, or giving it a start date or a deadline, asks Create Copy Now? Create Copy makes the next copy at once, makes your change on that copy’s task, and opens the copy. The template stays as it was and moves on to the date after. One Undo takes it all back.

The Repeat menu

Right-click a repeating task, or a repeating project’s template, and choose Repeat (on iPhone and iPad, open it and tap ⋯; on iPhone you can also tap a task’s rule). It has:

  • Edit Rule… to change the schedule.
  • Show Previous Copy to go to the latest copy.
  • Create Next Copy to make the next copy now, on its day, and move the template on.
  • Pause to stop making copies for a while; the rule then says “paused”, and a paused project leaves Scheduled. Resume starts it again, making any copy that has come due.
  • Stop… to end the repeat, after asking: the template and the copies it has made become ordinary tasks or projects, and no more copies are made.

The banner on a project’s template has Show Previous Copy and Create Next Copy too.

What else affects a repeat

  • Completing a project, or archiving a heading, stops the repeating tasks in it, after saying so; they go to Completed as ordinary tasks.
  • Duplicating a repeating task, a repeating project or any copy gives an ordinary task or project. A template’s menu has no Duplicate.
  • Deleting a project’s template asks first: the template and all future copies go, and copies already made stay.
  • When a repeat reaches its end, after its last copy or on its end date, the template goes to Completed.

Importing them

Import… brings in repeating tasks and repeating projects from a CSV file, through its repeat columns, and the script for Things 3 writes them for you, starting each one at the copy still open in Things (such as today’s), so that copy comes across too. A repeating project comes in as its template, always as a new project, and if its first date is today or earlier, today’s copy is made as it comes in. A task inside it can’t have a start date, deadline or repeat of its own. The columns and an example are on Import from other apps.

Reminders

A task can have a reminder: a date and time at which you’re alerted. My Tasks can’t send alerts itself, so the reminders ring through Apple Reminders: the calendar helper on your Mac adds each one to a Reminders list, and Apple Reminders alerts you on your iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Watch. It’s Mac only for now: it needs a Mac running the helper. The Reminder field shows on tasks once the helper is set up with a Reminders list (Calendar events).

Setting one

  • Click the bell on an open task, choose Reminder… in its menu, or press Cmd Shift R (R in a browser tab). With several tasks selected, the reminder goes on all of them.
  • Type a date and time, such as “tomorrow 9am”, “fri 2:30pm”, “jan 12 14:00”, “9pm” (today) or “in 30 minutes”, and press Return; a date alone means 9:00 AM. Or choose Later Today, Tomorrow or On the start date, or pick a day in the calendar and set the time.
  • The task’s card shows the reminder as a blue chip with a bell, and its row a small bell. Clear Reminder removes it.
  • On a repeating task you set only a time, and each copy reminds at that time on its own day.

When it rings

  • A new or changed reminder reaches Apple Reminders at the helper’s next reading, so up to its interval later (5 to 60 minutes), and only while your Mac is awake. A reminder set for a few minutes from now can be late.
  • To have a reminder ring as an alarm, mark it Urgent in Reminders. My Tasks leaves it as you set it.
  • Ticking the reminder in Reminders completes its task in My Tasks (Calendar events has the details).
  • Reminders come across when you import from Todoist or from Things 3 (Import from other apps).

Calendar events

My Tasks can show the events from your Mac’s calendars in Today and in Scheduled, on all your devices. A small helper on your Mac reads the calendars you choose and puts the events in your gist, encrypted with your passphrase like your tasks. Events are read-only: click or tap one to see its time, place, link, attendees and notes. Without the helper, no events show.

You need your Mac, the GitHub token and passphrase you use for My Tasks, and about ten minutes.

  1. Open Terminal (in Applications › Utilities).
  2. Paste these three lines and press Return. They make a folder for the helper in your home folder, download its two files there (read calendar_sync.py and reminders.py on GitHub), and install the one package it needs for the encryption, for your user only (no admin rights needed). The same lines update a helper you already have. 
    mkdir -p ~/MyTasksHelper && cd ~/MyTasksHelper
curl -fsSLO https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CyberAdvisor/MyTasks/main/helper/calendar_sync.py -O https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CyberAdvisor/MyTasks/main/helper/reminders.py
python3 -m pip install --user cryptography
    If your Mac offers to install the command line developer tools, install them (they include Python) and paste the lines again.
  3. Run the setup and answer its questions: your token, then the passphrase of each of your gists in turn, each asked for by the gist's name (press Return to skip one; a passphrase you’ve already typed is tried on the next gists first), then the calendars to show in each gist (press Return for all, or 0 for none) and how many months ahead besides this one, for all gists (1 to 6; press Return for 4), and last the Reminders list for your tasks’ reminders, for all gists (a number for one of your lists, Return for a list named My Tasks, made if you don’t have one, or 0 to leave reminders off). If macOS asks whether Terminal may access your calendars or your reminders, click Allow. Your token and passphrases are kept in your Mac’s Keychain. 
    python3 calendar_sync.py setup
    At the end it prints a start line. Copy it.
  4. Open Shortcuts, create a new shortcut named My Tasks Calendar, add the Run Shell Script action, and paste the start line into it. It ends in --every 15: the helper reads your calendars every 15 minutes (5 to 60) and writes only when something changed.
  5. Run the shortcut. The first time, macOS asks whether Shortcuts may access your calendars, and, with a Reminders list chosen, your reminders: click Allow each time. The helper then keeps running in the background.
  6. Optional: so it starts again after your Mac restarts, add an automation in Shortcuts › Automation: Time of Day, daily, running My Tasks Calendar. Running it while the helper is already running just replaces it.

Within a few minutes the events appear in My Tasks: in Today, under the title; in Scheduled, above each of the next seven days’ tasks, and then listed by day for the rest of this month and each month after it (a repeating event once, with how many times it occurs). Until the helper has written to a gist, that gist’s Settings › Calendar Events says only that calendar events and reminders need the helper on a Mac, with a link here: allow up to the helper’s interval after setup, plus the app’s next sync. Then it shows when the helper last wrote them, and hides or shows them on all your devices. Settings › Sync adds Calendar helper last updated: the last time the helper wrote to this gist. It writes only when something changed, so on a quiet calendar that time can be days old while the helper is running. In Terminal, in the helper’s folder, python3 calendar_sync.py status says whether it’s running and when it last wrote; stop stops it.

With a Reminders list chosen, each open task with a reminder gets a reminder in that list, at its next reading (so up to its interval later, and only while your Mac is awake), and Apple Reminders brings it to your iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Watch. A repeating task gets one repeating reminder. Settings › Calendar Events then says reminders are on, and the Reminder field appears on tasks. Changing a task’s title or reminder changes the reminder; completing the task completes it, and canceling, trashing or deleting the task, or clearing its reminder, removes it. Anything else you set in Reminders, such as Urgent, is left as you set it.

Ticking a reminder in Reminders completes its task in My Tasks, at the time you ticked it. It arrives after the helper’s next reading and the app’s next sync, and the app says so: Completed in Reminders, with Undo to reopen the task. For a repeating task, the copy for that day is completed. Unticking a reminder in Reminders doesn’t reopen its task, and deleting one doesn’t complete it. A task you reopen in My Tasks stays open.

If you change a gist’s passphrase in My Tasks, or add a gist, run the setup again on your Mac. Until then the helper doesn’t write to that gist, and that gist’s Settings › Calendar Events says so; your other gists keep getting events.

Back up and restore

Your gist is where every device's tasks are kept; a backup file is a separate copy that you keep yourself.

  • Save Backup in Settings › Backup downloads a file of your tasks, projects, areas, tags, contexts, saved searches and synced settings. On iPhone and iPad, choose Save to Files in the share sheet. The file is not encrypted: keep it somewhere safe.
  • Restore from Backup… replaces all your tasks with the file's, on this device, in your gist and, once they sync, on your other devices. Anything created since the backup is deleted. It can't be undone, so the confirmation offers Save Backup First.
  • Restore needs a working sync connection. Your theme, token, passphrase and gist connection don't change.

Import… in Settings › Import From Other Apps adds tasks from a CSV file, such as one from another app, to what you have. It shows what the file holds first, and one Undo takes it all back. The file’s columns, a template and a sample are on Import from other apps.

Archive

Settings › Archive keeps your gist small. It saves completed and canceled tasks and projects finished more than a number of days ago (90 unless you change it) to a new CSV file, then removes them from My Tasks on every device. A completed project goes with its headings and tasks, and checklists go with their tasks; repeating tasks stay. It asks first, and if the file isn’t saved nothing is removed. Removing is final, like Empty Trash, so keep the file: Import… can bring items back from it.

Importing from Todoist

Import… reads Todoist’s own export file, one project per file, so you can move your projects across without any other tools. It works on any device that can pick a file.

Moving a project across

  1. In Todoist on the web or the desktop app, open the project, click its ⋯ menu, and choose Import & export › Export to CSV.
  2. Untick Use relative dates, so dates are written as dates (“Nov 26”) and not as “+1 day”, then click Export. Do this for each project you want.
  3. In My Tasks, open Settings › Import From Other Apps › Import… and choose the file, or several of them at once.
  4. The preview says it read a Todoist export, names the project each file makes, and says how many tasks it holds. Click Import. One Undo takes it all back.
  5. Check the project against Todoist. If anything came across wrong, report it as an issue on GitHub, with the line from the file if you can.

What comes across

  • Each file becomes a project named after the file, with no area: drag it onto an area afterwards. The project’s description becomes its notes. A project with the same name as one you have is added to.
  • Sections become headings. Labels written in a task’s name (“@phone”) become tags.
  • Priorities p1 to p3 become P1 to P3; p4, Todoist’s “no priority”, becomes none.
  • A task’s description becomes its notes, and each comment is added to them with its date.
  • Dates, deadlines, reminders and repeats such as “every friday”, “every other week”, “every 1st”, “every 2nd tue” and “every! 3 days” (counted from completion). A repeat starts at its next date from today.
  • A sub-task becomes a task of its own, under the same heading, with “Sub-task of:” and its parent’s name in its notes.

Limits

  • English only. Dates and repeats are read only from a file exported in English. A date My Tasks can’t read, or one in another language, is left off the task and kept as text in its notes (“Todoist date: …”); the preview says how many.
  • Times of day are dropped, as tasks have no times.
  • Reminders are brought in, read as a date and time (on a repeating task, its time only). Todoist doesn’t document how its file writes them, so one My Tasks can’t read, and one set as a time before the due date, is kept as text in the task’s notes (“Todoist reminder: …”). A reminder rings only once the calendar helper on your Mac is set up with a Reminders list (Calendar events). A deadline on a repeating task is kept as text.
  • Completed tasks, durations, assignees and section descriptions aren’t in the file or aren’t brought in.
  • Todoist doesn’t write a repeat’s next date in the file, so a repeating task starts again from today.

How many tasks

Each gist holds up to 5,000 tasks, counting completed tasks and the Trash, in a file of up to 5 MB, whichever comes first. Tasks with long notes or checklists can fill the 5 MB before 5,000 tasks. From 4,500 tasks or 4.5 MB, the bottom of the sidebar says how near you are. At 5,000 tasks, adding a task says there's no room, and keeps what you typed. Past 5 MB, the gist can't be updated: the sidebar says Sync error — too large, and your changes wait on the device until you make room.

  • To make room while keeping a record, use Archive: it saves old completed tasks to a file, then removes them.
  • Or empty the Trash, then delete completed tasks you no longer need: in Completed, select them (Cmd A selects them all) and press Delete, then empty the Trash.
  • To keep a record of them first, save a backup in Settings › Backup.
  • For the 5 MB, shorten very long notes too. Deleted items still take a little room until they're cleared, 180 days after deletion. Settings › Sync shows the file's size.
  • A repeating task still makes its next copy, and tasks from your other devices still arrive, even at the limit.
  • The limit is per gist: to go past it, split your tasks into separate gists, for example one for a large project.

Offline — changes saved on this deviceNothing: your changes are kept and sync when GitHub can be reached again.
